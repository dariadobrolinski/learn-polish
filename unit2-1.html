Greetings & Farewells

Unit 2, Section 1

Formal Greetings

  • Good morning/Good day: Dzień dobry
    • Pronounciation: jane doh-brih
    • Use: Can be used all throughout the day. It's polite to use when entering a shop or greeting people you don't know well.
    • Example: Say you are walking into a bakery. To greet the employee(s), you would say "dzień dobry".

  • Good evening: Dobry wieczór
    • Pronounciation: doh-brih vyeh-choor
    • Use: Used in the evening in the same way dzień dobry is used. In the evening, you can use either this or "dzień dobry". Either works.
    • Example: Say you are walking into a bakery, in the evening. To greet the employee(s), you would say "dobry wieczór".

  • I welcome you: Witam
    • Pronounciation: vee-tahm
      • Use: Often used when greeting guests or attendees.
    • Example: Say you are speaking at a conference, you would greet the crowd by saying "witam"

Formal Farewells

  • Goodbye: Do widzenia
    • Prounciation: doh vee-dzen-ya
    • Use: You can use this in virtually every situation which doesn't call for a fully informal farewell.
    • Example: After a taxi driver drops you off, you would say "do widziena" to them.

  • Goodnight: Dobranoc
    • Pronounciation: doh-brah-nots
    • Use: When you are parting ways with someone in the evening, this applies to more formal encounters. BUTTTTT, you can also say this to wish someone a good night's sleep. In this case, the formaility of the occasion doesn't matter.
    • Example: Say you and your boss went out for dinner. Since this is a more formal situation and you are parting ways at night, you would say "dobranoc".

Informal Greetings

  • Hello: Cześć
    • Pronounciation: che-sh-ch
    • Use: Often said to close family, friends, or your peers. It is not acceptable to say cześć to your professor or to the cashier at a supermarket.
    • Example: If you were to meet up with some friends, you could greet them by saying, "cześć".

  • Hey: Hej/Hejka
    • Pronounciation: hey/hey-ka
    • Use: Used in informal settings when you feel like you need something more relaxed or casual than the usual cześć.
    • Example: If you're friend were to come over, you could say "hej" or "hejka".

  • What's up: Siema
    • Pronounciation: sh-eh-ma
    • Use: Very informal, laid-back greeting. Used mostly among youth.
    • Example: You can use this greeting with good friends. If you meet you're good friends out for dinner, you can say "siema".

Informal Farewells

  • Bye: Cześć
    • Pronounciation: che-sh-ch
    • Use: You may be shocked to see cześć again and this time as a farewell. Cześć can be used as both a greeting and farewell. Both uses are informal and should be only used among close family and friends.
    • Example: If you had a friendly get-together and people are leaving, you can say "cześć".

  • See you later: Do zobaczenia
    • Pronounciation: doh zoh-bah-chien-ya
    • Use: This is the less formal version of "do widzenia". This should be used among friends or family you think you will see soon.
    • Example: If you and a friend are parting ways and have plans on Monday, you can say "do zobaczenia". You can also add when you will see them. For example, if you are seeing them Monday you can say "do zobaczenia w poniedzałek".

  • Bye/Bye-bye: Pa/Pa-pa
    • Pronounciation: pah/pah-pah
    • Use: This is more intimate than the standard "cześć". "Pa-pa" is more informal than just "pa" and is also often seen as childish like "bye-bye" is in English. You would use this among people very close to you.
    • Example: You are saying bye to your girlfriend, you can say "pa" or "pa-pa".

  • So long: Na razie
    • Pronounciation: nah rah-z-yeh
    • Use: It might, but doesn't have to, communicate the intention of seeing each other in the indefinite future. A shortened version for na razie is nara .
    • Example: When you are parting ways with close friends you can use "na razie" or "nara"

  • See you tomorrow: Do jutra
    • Pronounciation: doh yu-trah
    • Use: Used when you intend to see the person again tomorrow.
    • Example: If you are parting ways with your girlfriend and you know you will see her again tomorrow, you could say "do jutra". You can also add the day of the week. For example, you know you will see your girlfriend Monday, you can say "do poniedziałku".
