Numbers

Unit 2, Section 2

Now we are going to learn how to count in Polish and how to use numbers in sentences where gender matters.


Counting in Polish

Below are numbers 1-10 in Polish:

  1. Jeden (yeh-den)
  2. Dwa (dv-ah)
  3. Trzy (tch-ih)
  4. Cztery (chteh-rih)
  5. Pięć (pyeh-nch)
  6. Sześć (sheh-shch)
  7. Siedem (shyeh-dem)
  8. Osiem (oh-shem)
  9. Dziewięć (jyeh-vyen-ch)
  10. Dziesięć (jyeh-shen-ch)

To form numbers beyond 10:

  • 11-19: Add "-naście" (nah-sh-cheh) to the base of 1-9.
    • Example: Jedenaście (11), dwanaście (12), trzynaście (13).
  • 20, 30, etc: Add "dzieści(a)" (jeh-sh-chi or jeh-sh-chiah) to the base number.
    • Example: Dwadzieścia (20), trzydieści (30), czterdieści (40).


Feminine Vs. Masculine Numbers in Polish

In Polish, the form of a number can change based on the grammatical gender of the noun it describes. Below is a quick guide for feminine, masculine and neuter forms:

For Singular Nouns (1):

  • Masculine: Use jeden (e.g., jeden chłopak = one boy)
  • Feminine: Use jedna (e.g., jedna dziewczynka = one girl)
  • Neuter: Use jedno (e.g., jedno dziecko = one child)

For Plural Nouns:

  • Masculine personal nouns (referring to people):
    • 2: Use dwóch (e.g., dwóch mężczyźni = two men)
    • 3: Use trzech (e.g., trzech chołpaców = three boys)
    • 4: Use czterech (e.g., czterech mężczyźn = four men)
  • Masculine nouns (non-personal):
    • Just use the number name (e.g., dwa stoły = two tables)
  • Feminine and neuter personal nouns:
    • 2: dwie (e.g., dwie kobiety = two women).
    • For feminine and neuter nouns, 3 and 4 would just be trzy and cztery.
  • For numbers 5 and beyond (all genders), just use the number.
