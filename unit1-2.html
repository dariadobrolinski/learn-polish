Polish Diacritics

Unit 1, Section 2


A diacritic a sign that represents accents.


Polish Diacritics

  • Ogonek: This means little tail and is responsible for nasalizing vowel sounds (ą, ę).
  • Kreska: This means line and it either means palatalization as in ś or ć or turns o into a u sound (ó).
  • Kropka: This means dot and is only used with the letter ż (makes like a rz sound)
  • Kreską Ukośną: The slash across letters is only used with ł (makes a w sound).

Words With These Diacritics

  • Ć - like 'ch' as in 'chomp'. When placed next to a vowel, it is spelt 'ci' but pronounced the same.
    • Example: Used mostly in infinitive verbs like spać (spah-ch) meaning "to sleep".

  • Ś - this is like 'sh' like in 'shut'. When placed next to a vowel, it is spelt 'si' but pronounced the same.
    • Example: Like ślub (sh-lu-b) meaning "wedding" and siebie (sh-eh-b-ye) meaning "yourself".

  • Ń - this is a palatized n, similar to the Spanish ñ, but also you will find the sound in the word 'onion'. When placed next to a vowel, it is spelt 'ni' but pronounced the same.
    • Example: Like the town in Poland, Poznań.

  • Ź - There is no English equivalent. It is very similar to Ż, but softer. When placed next to a vowel, it is spelt 'zi' but pronounced the same.
    • Example: Źle (badly).

  • Ó - Pronounced like the English 'oo', as in cook. It is pronounced the same way a Polish u is.
    • Example: Król (kr-oo-l) meaning "king".

  • Ż - Similarly pronounced like the 's' in the English word 'measure', 'rz'.
    • Example: Żyrafa (rzy-rah-fa) meaning "giraffe".

  • Ą - The sound represented by this letter is like a nasaly O. It sounds more or less like the 'on' in 'wrong'.
      Example: Pączki (pon-ch-key) meaning "Polish doughnuts".

  • Ę - A nasal E. This letter is mostly found as the end of a first person singular conjugation. When found in the middle of a word, it makes more of a 'in' sound like in the word 'thin'.
      Example: Dziękuję (jen-koo-yeh) meaning "thank you".

  • Ł - Pronounced like 'w' in 'wool'.
      Example: Ładnie (whad-nyeh) meaning pretty or nicely.
